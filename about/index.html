About Flexibility Lens

A short diagnostic that helps project managers and team members see which criteria they use to judge cognitive flexibility, and how those criteria differ from their colleagues'.

What it is

What makes someone flexible when a project has to change course? Professionals answer this question with different criteria. For some, flexible means seeing many alternatives; for others, it means noticing that the situation changed and acting quickly. Flexibility Lens shows each person which criteria they use, what their view tends to overlook, and which views would complement it in a team.

The diagnostic describes what a person values in a flexible decision-maker. It does not measure how flexible that person is.

How it works

  1. Trade-offs

    Seven to thirteen choices between two hypothetical people who differ on two dimensions only, using the level descriptions from the study's instrument. One repeated question checks consistency.

  2. Weights

    Each answer is a constraint on the weight of the four dimensions. The app averages the weights that best fit all answers, in the spirit of the PAPRIKA method, with the linear value functions the study found.

  3. Profile

    Your weights are compared with the average of each of the seven profiles (Manhattan distance, as in the study's cluster analysis) to find the nearest one and its opposite.

  4. Self-check and team view

    Four optional items compare what you value with how you see yourself. A short result code lets a team put its members' views side by side.

The research behind it

Flexibility Lens is based on the doctoral thesis of Lidiane da Silva Souza, Cognitive flexibility as a microfoundation of strategic adaptation: ontologies, architecture and hierarchy of its dimensions, in the Graduate Program in Business Administration (PPGA) at Universidade Presbiteriana Mackenzie, advised by Fellipe Silva Martins.

The thesis breaks cognitive flexibility into four dimensions: Awareness of Alternatives (what), Processing Agility (how), Context Sensitivity (where and when) and Agentic Disposition (who). Its third article (Souza & Martins, in progress) asked 210 professionals who work in project teams to weigh these dimensions with the PAPRIKA pairwise method, between June and September 2026. On average the four dimensions weigh about the same, but almost nobody thinks like the average: the participants split into seven profiles, each giving priority to a pair of dimensions.

The clustering has modest quality (mean silhouette 0.256), so the profiles are best read as tendencies, not fixed types. The idea that teams mixing opposite profiles cover flexibility better is a hypothesis from the study, not a tested finding.

Who makes it

Flexibility Lens is by Fellipe Silva Martins (Lattes) and Lidiane da Silva Souza, developed at the Laboratório de IA + Estratégia of the Escola de Negócios, Universidade Presbiteriana Mackenzie. It turns the results of Lidiane's doctoral research, advised by Fellipe, into a tool that people in project teams can use.
